## 4. The project in one minute

**One line:** LIFELINE is a Marswalk planner with one job: keep the crew within reach of home, even if the rover or a suit fails.

**Challenge:** Interplanetary Survival Guide: Martian Map. The task is a layered map, built from several missions' data, for planning a safe Marswalk that does new science.

**Problem:** On Mars no rescue team can come. A crew far from the habitat must be able to get home on its own, whatever fails. A normal map tells you where to go, not whether you can get back.

**Idea:**
- Apollo planners had one rule: never drive farther from the lunar module than you can walk back if the rover fails.
- They drew it in 1970 as a fixed 3.1 km circle before the mission.
- LIFELINE turns that rule into a live check, at every minute of the plan, from every 30 m cell of real Martian terrain.

**The three limits:**
1. **If the rover fails (walk-back limit):** the suit reserve left must cover the walk home over the real terrain, plus a 20% safety pad.
2. **If a suit fails (ride-back limit):** 57 min of emergency oxygen must cover the ride home at 10 km/h, plus the 20% pad.
3. **Time and daylight:** home within a 6 h EVA, starting after sunrise and ending before sunset (05:52 to 17:22 local time).

**Gates:** leaving each stop, the tool checks the margin ahead. If it is under 30 min, or the plan would run past the time limit:
- **Plan B** drops the lowest-science stop.
- If that isn't enough, **plan C** sends the crew home.
- The 20% pad is never lowered.

**Verdicts:**

| Verdict | Colour | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **GO FOR EVA** | green | All limits clear. |
| **GO, MARGIN TIGHT** | yellow | All limits hold, but one is near its edge. |
| **NO-GO** | red | A limit is broken. The tool lists the changes that would fix it. |

**The mission:**
- The site is the boxwork ridges of Mount Sharp, Gale Crater, where Curiosity is exploring now.
- **Habitat:** on Curiosity's sol 3038 position (flat ground).
- **Stop A, Altadena:** where Curiosity drilled on sol 4564.
- **Stop B, Monte Grande:** the "Valle de la Luna" hollow Curiosity drilled.
- **Stop C:** a hollow inside the boxwork that no rover has reached. Curiosity's path passes about 60 m away. "This is why a human goes."
- Visit order is always A → B → C.

**Who uses it:**
- The person who plans the crew's walk (EVA), before and during the walk.
- The Walk view is the same model, cut down to a wrist display the crew can read with gloves on.

## 5. Numbers you may use

All numbers are for the default values. Every one comes from the project's tests (`tests/audit-numbers.js`).

**Plan:**
- Verdict **GO FOR EVA**.
- The plan takes **2 h 57**: out at 08:00, home at 10:57.
- All three stops visited.
- Route **10.2 km**, **492 m** of climb.
- Time spare **3 h 03** against the 6 h EVA.
- Worst rover-failure margin **32 min**, while working at C at T+2:14.
- Worst suit-failure margin **15 min**, at B.

**Testing:**
- Every crisis tested at every minute: **178 minutes** per crisis, **356 single failures** in total.
- Every single failure leaves a way home at every minute.

| Crisis | Worst margin | Unsafe minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Rover fails | 32 min | 0 |
| Suit fails | 15 min | 0 |
| Solar storm | 23 min | 0 |
| Crew injured (rover works) | over 8 h | 0 |
| Rover fails + suit fails | **−203 min** | 158 of 178 |

- **Double failures are honestly not covered**, as in Apollo, which also planned for one failure at a time.
- The tool shows exactly where a double failure breaks the plan.
- Covering double failures needs a second rover or supply caches on the route. That is a next step.
- NASA's own suit plan describes a second rover that can rescue the crew (EVA-EXP-0042, p. 41).

**Running late at stop A:**

| Late at stop A | Gate decision | Home by | Worst walk-back margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| on time | Plan A | T+2:57 | 32 min |
| 15 min | Plan B (skip B) | T+2:40 | 45 min |
| 45 min | Plan C (go home) | T+2:34 | 66 min |

**Validation:**
- **10 of 10** checks pass.
- The model reproduces Apollo's walking radius: **3.12 km** vs 3.1 km.
- The terrain matches Curiosity's own altitudes at **885** points: RMSE **11.7 m** over 823 m of relief.
- All **35,139** terrain cells match the original ESA file within 0.1 m.
- Adding a crisis never raises the margin (4,984 comparisons).

**Route sanity:**
- Steepest slope on the route **16°** (limit 25°). Only **0.18 km** of the 10.2 km is above 15°.
- Slopes count: the walk home from A takes **196 min, not the 134 min** a straight line would suggest.
- **41%** of the route is within 100 m of ground Curiosity actually drove.

**Sensitivity:**
- 20% less suit capacity cuts the margin to **−44 min**.
- 20% slower walking cuts it to **−31 min**.
- Either one turns today's plan to NO-GO.

**Environment:**
- Radiation **0.64 ± 0.12 mSv/day** (Curiosity RAD). **0.08 mSv** on this walk.
- Daylight 05:52 to 17:22.
- Air **−82 to −5 °C**, pressure **679 to 782 Pa** (Curiosity REMS, 123 sols).

**Default inputs** (all 15 are editable on the site):
- Walking 2 km/h.
- Suit oxygen and cooling water 8 h each.
- Emergency oxygen 57 min.
- Pad 20%.
- Gate 30 min.
- Payload 10 kg.
- Slope limit 25°.
- Rover 8 km/h, 10 km/h in an emergency.
- 30 min at each stop.
- Max EVA 6 h.
- Start 08:00.
- Storm warning 30 min.

**Where the values come from:**
- **Apollo 15 planning memo, NASA-CR-111182 (1970), pp. 1–2:**
  - activity rates
  - the 20% pad
  - rover speeds
  - 57 min of emergency oxygen
  - the 3.1 km radius
- **NASA xEVA (Artemis) ConOps, EVA-EXP-0042 Rev B (2020), pp. 29, 36, 40:**
  - walking 2 km/h
  - 8 h suit
  - 6 h EVA
- **Curiosity REMS:** daylight.

**Team choices** (labelled on the site, not sourced):
- 30 min per stop
- 08:00 start
- the slope slowdown
- the 30 min gate
- the 25° slope limit
- the injury factor
- the storm warning time
- the shelter rule

## 7. How to record the live site

- Use Chrome on a desktop with the window at **1440×900 or larger**. Zoom the browser so the full Plan view fits, and hide the bookmarks bar.
- Record at 1080p or higher, with the cursor visible and highlighted.
- Reload before each take so the defaults are back. The time slider starts near T+0:40.
- **Stops** can only be dragged on the 2D map, not in 3D.
- **Crisis buttons** sit under the map: Rover fails, Suit failure, Crew injured, Solar storm, Running late +15 min, Clear all. The crisis buttons can be combined.
- Press **Clear all** between scenes.
- **Views** are switched by the Plan / Walk / 3D buttons at the top. The verdict badge is in the top right.

**3D controls:**
- Overview / Crew view / Top buttons.
- Height slider.
- Realistic Mars / Safety Overlay toggle.
- Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom.
- The first press of 3D takes a few seconds to load.
