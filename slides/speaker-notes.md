# LIFELINE: speaker notes

About 45 seconds per slide, 5 minutes in total. Say the slide title first, then the points. Keep the live site open in a second tab for questions.

## 1. Title
- "LIFELINE is a Marswalk planner. Its one job: keep the crew within reach of home, even when the rover or a suit fails."
- "It runs on real Mars terrain at the boxwork ridges of Mount Sharp, where Curiosity is driving now."
- "The picture is the live tool: the crew, the route home, the habitat."

## 2. The problem
- "On the Moon, Apollo crews could count on each other and on a short trip home. On Mars, no rescue can come."
- "Our user is the person who plans a crewed walk. Their decision: GO or NO-GO, and what to do the moment something fails."
- "A map tells you where to go. It does not tell you whether you can still get back."
- "These are our three stops: two places Curiosity drilled, and stop C, which no rover has reached. That is why a human goes."

## 3. The idea
- "Apollo planners had one rule: never go farther than you can walk home. We turned it into a live check at every minute."
- Point at the three cards: "If the rover fails, the suit must cover the walk home with a 20% pad. If a suit fails, 57 minutes of emergency oxygen must cover the ride home. And the crew must be home within a 6-hour EVA and before sunset."
- "The Apollo numbers come from the 1970 Apollo 15 planning memo, page 1. Walking speed and suit capacity come from NASA's Artemis suit plan. Sunset comes from Curiosity's weather station."
- "At every stop a gate checks the margin ahead. If it is too small, the plan drops a stop or sends the crew home. It never lowers the safety pad."

## 4. The tool
- "Here the rover fails at T+1:30. LIFELINE says: walk home now, 1 hour 2 minutes to spare."
- "The map colours every 30-metre cell: green safe, yellow tight, red no return. It shrinks as the suits run down."
- "On the right: the margin if the rover fails here, if the suit fails here, and the plan ladder."
- "The same model on a wrist display, big enough to read with a glove."

## 5. The 3D view
- "The 3D view shows what a flat map cannot: the climbs, and the edge of safe ground."
- "The teal wall is the safety fence: beyond it, the crew could not walk home if the rover failed now. Move the time slider and it closes in."
- "The profile shows the climbs along the route: 10.2 km, 492 metres of climb, which cost time and oxygen."
- "The shape is real HRSC data. The colours and fine texture are illustrative; we say so on the screen."

## 6. Tested and validated
- "We started every crisis at every minute of the plan. Every single failure leaves a way home. Two failures at once do not, as in Apollo, and the tool shows exactly where."
- "The model reproduces Apollo's own 3.1 km walking radius. The terrain matches Curiosity's own altitudes within 11.7 metres. We checked all 35,139 terrain cells against the original file."
- "Data from three spacecraft and two agencies: ESA's Mars Express, NASA's Curiosity and MRO."

## 7. Impact and next steps
- "The same check works on the Moon: our defaults come from NASA's Artemis suit plan."
- "Next, connect it to suit, rover and space-weather data, so the crew gets the order the moment a failure is detected."
- "We are open about what is still a choice: these values are labelled on the page, and the sensitivity chart shows what they cost."
- "Try it yourself; the link is on the slide. Thank you."
