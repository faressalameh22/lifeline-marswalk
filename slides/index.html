The problem

On Mars no rescue can come, so the planner must know at every minute whether the crew can still get home.

The user

The person who plans a crewed walk (EVA) on Mars.

The decision

GO or NO-GO for a walk to three science stops, and what to do the moment something fails.

The gap

A map shows where to go. It does not show whether the crew can still get back if the rover breaks down or a suit fails.

The idea

We turned Apollo's rule, "never go farther than you can walk home", into a live check on real Mars terrain.

If the rover fails

Walk home

The suit reserve left must cover the walk home over the real terrain, plus a 20% safety pad.

Apollo 15 planning memo, NASA-CR-111182 (1970), p. 1

If a suit fails

Ride home

57 min of emergency oxygen must cover the ride home at 10 km/h, plus the pad.

Apollo 15 planning memo, p. 1

Always

Time and daylight

Home within a 6 h EVA and before sunset.

NASA xEVA ConOps EVA-EXP-0042 (2020); sunset from Curiosity REMS

Every minute, every place

Walking time home is computed from every 30 m cell of the HRSC terrain (slower on slopes). The reserve runs down with Apollo activity rates. Walking 2 km/h and an 8 h suit come from NASA's Artemis suit plan.

Gates at every stop

Habitat → A → B → C → home. If the margin ahead drops under 30 min, the plan drops a stop (B) or sends the crew home (C). The safety pad is never lowered.

Impact and next steps

The same tested check can protect crews on the Moon and on Mars, from planning to live operations.

Ready for the Moon too

The defaults come from NASA's Artemis suit plan; any site with an elevation model works the same way.

From planning to operations

Today: margins at every minute and "what if" crises. Next: feed it suit, rover and space-weather data, so the crew gets the order the moment a failure is detected.

Next steps

  • Second rover or supply caches for double failures
  • Mars suit data when it exists; THEMIS sand layer
  • A user study with EVA planners

What is still a choice

30 min per stop, 08:00 start, the slope slowdown, the injury factor, the storm warning time and the shelter rule are team choices, labelled on the page. They matter: 20% less suit capacity turns today's plan to NO-GO, and the sensitivity chart shows it.
