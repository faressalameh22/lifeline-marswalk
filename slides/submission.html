LIFELINE

Never go farther than you can walk home.

A Marswalk planner that checks, at every minute and on real terrain, that the crew can still get home if the rover or a suit fails.

What it is

A free web tool (no login, no install) that plans a crewed walk (EVA) to three science stops in the boxwork ridges of Mount Sharp, Gale Crater, where Curiosity is driving now.

Who it is for

The person who plans the walk. It answers one decision: GO or NO-GO, and what the crew must do the moment something fails.

How it works

Apollo's walk-back rule, computed live from 30 m ESA terrain, NASA Apollo and Artemis planning values and Curiosity weather data.

Result

Today's plan: GO FOR EVA, 2 h 57. 356 single failures simulated, one at every minute: every one leaves a way home.

The problem and the mission

A map shows where to go, but not whether the crew can still get back. On Mars no rescue can come.

Why it matters

On the Moon, Apollo crews were never more than a short walk from the lander, and planners drew a fixed safe radius before each EVA. On Mars, a crew far from the habitat with a broken rover or a failing suit has only what is left in the suit. The planner must know, minute by minute, whether that is enough.

The decision

Before the walk: GO or NO-GO, and which stops fit. During it: if the rover fails, a suit fails, someone is hurt, a solar storm is announced or the crew runs late, what to do now.

The mission (default plan)

Out at 08:00 from a habitat on Curiosity's sol 3038 position. Habitat → A → B → C → home by rover, 30 min of work at each stop. 10.2 km, 492 m of climb.

Science value rises from A to C: A and B extend Curiosity's drill record; C is new ground that only a human visit can sample. That is why a crew walks here, and why their safety must be checked at every step.

How it works

Three limits, checked at every minute of the plan, from every 30 m cell of real terrain.

1 Terrain

ESA HRSC elevation of Gale Crater, 30 m cells (159 × 221).

2 Walk home

Shortest walking time home from every cell (8-direction search), slower on slopes, no walking above 25°.

3 Reserve

Suit oxygen and water run down minute by minute at Apollo's riding, working and walking rates.

4 Margins

At each minute: reserve left minus padded walk home; emergency oxygen minus padded ride home; time left.

5 Decision

GO / NO-GO, the tightest moment, the reason, and gates that change the plan.

If the rover fails

Walk-back limit

The suit reserve left must cover the walk home over the real terrain, plus a 20% safety pad.

If a suit fails

Ride-back limit

57 min of emergency oxygen must cover the ride home at 10 km/h, plus the 20% pad.

Always

Time and daylight

Home within a 6 h EVA, starting after sunrise and ending before sunset (05:52 to 17:22 local time).

Gates and verdict

Leaving each stop, the tool checks the margin ahead. Under 30 min, or past the time limit: plan B drops the lowest-science stop; if that is not enough, plan C sends the crew home. The 20% pad is never lowered. GO: all limits clear. GO, MARGIN TIGHT: all clear but one is near its edge. NO-GO: a limit is broken, with the fixes that would clear it.

Where the numbers come from

Activity rates, 20% pad, rover 8 / 10 km/h, 57 min emergency oxygen: Apollo 15 planning memo, NASA-CR-111182 (1970), p. 1. Walking 2 km/h, 8 h suit, 6 h EVA: NASA xEVA (Artemis) ConOps, EVA-EXP-0042 Rev B (2020), pp. 29, 36, 40. Daylight: Curiosity REMS. Team choices (30 min per stop, 08:00 start, slope slowdown, 30 min gate, 25° slope limit) are labelled in the tool.

Is the route sensible?

Steepest slope 16° (limit 25°); only 0.18 of 10.2 km is above 15°. Slopes are counted: the walk home from A takes 196 min, not the 134 min a straight line would give. 41% of the route is within 100 m of ground Curiosity actually drove.

How sensitive is it?

20% less suit capacity cuts the margin to −44 min; 20% slower walking to −31 min. Either turns today's plan to NO-GO, and the tool's sensitivity chart shows it.

Data, limits and impact

Built on data from three spacecraft and two agencies, honest about its limits, ready for the Moon and Mars.

Data used

  • Terrain: HRSC 30 m DTM of Gale Crater, ESA Mars Express (Persaud et al. 2021, Zenodo, CC BY 4.0)
  • Curiosity: 885 waypoints (habitat, A, B, terrain check), Mastcam and Hazcam images, REMS weather and daylight, RAD radiation
  • MRO HiRISE image of stop C (NASA/JPL-Caltech/University of Arizona)
  • Apollo 15 planning memo (1970) and NASA's Artemis suit plan (2020)

Limits

30 m cells cannot see boulders or soft sand; a real walk would recheck the route with HiRISE terrain (about 1 m) and a THEMIS sand layer. No Mars suit exists yet, so suit values come from NASA's Artemis (Moon) plan. 30 min per stop, the 08:00 start, the slope slowdown, the injury factor, the storm warning and the shelter rule are team choices, labelled in the tool. Not yet tested with real EVA planners.

Impact and next steps

  • Works on the Moon or any site with an elevation model; defaults already follow NASA's Artemis plan
  • Next: feed it live suit, rover and space-weather data so the crew gets the order the moment a failure is detected
  • Second rover or supply caches to cover double failures; a user study with EVA planners
