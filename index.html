Model and assumptions

walk_speed(cell) = flat_speed × e^(−0.045·slope°) × sand_factor × load_factor impassable above the slope limit walk_home(cell) = fastest walking time to the habitat over the terrain (Dijkstra, 8 directions) reserve(t) = min(oxygen, cooling water) − Σ activity_rate × minutes drive 0.67 · work 1.00 · walk 1.14 walk-back margin = reserve(t) ÷ walk_rate − (1 + pad) × walk_home if the rover fails ride-back margin = emergency_O2 − (1 + pad) × ride_home at emergency speed if the suit fails time limit = the whole plan ends inside the max time in suit GO = all three hold along the whole plan; gates: margin < gate → plan B (drop lowest science) → plan C (go home)

Terrain is the 30 m HRSC elevation map (ESA Mars Express), checked against Curiosity’s own altitudes in the validation panel; sand is not modelled until the THEMIS layer is connected. The two limits come from Apollo traverse planning. The walk-back limit protected the crew if the rover failed; the ride-back limit protected them if the backpack failed, using its emergency oxygen supply of about 0.95 h and an emergency rover speed of 10 km/h. Activity rates are the Apollo metabolic rates for riding, station work and walking (700, 1050 and 1200 BTU/h), scaled to station work. A 1970 Apollo planning study found the time-in-suit limit, then 5 h, usually capped riding traverses. The crew drives to the farthest stop first so the walk home shrinks as the suits run down. Walking speed on Mars, suit capacities, the payload effect (−0.5% speed and +1% effort per kg), the injured-crew factor (half speed, +30% effort), the 4.25 h suit limit used here and the storm warning time are team assumptions to be sourced. Lowering the safety pad is never offered as a fix.
