Validation: does the model work?

Sensitivity: tightest walk-back margin of the current plan when one assumption moves by ±20% (payload 0 to 20 kg, pad 10 to 30%). The white line is today's value; the longest bars are the numbers worth sourcing first.

Model and assumptions

walk_speed(cell) = flat_speed × e^(−0.045·slope°) × sand_factor × load_factor impassable above the slope limit [slope term: team assumption] walk_home(cell) = fastest walking time to the habitat over the terrain (Dijkstra, 8 directions) reserve(t) = min(oxygen, cooling water) − Σ activity_rate × minutes drive 0.67 · work 1.00 · walk 1.14 walk-back margin = reserve(t) ÷ walk_rate − (1 + pad) × walk_home if the rover fails ride-back margin = emergency_O2 − (1 + pad) × ride_home at emergency speed if the suit fails time limit = the whole plan ends inside the max time in suit GO = all three hold along the whole plan; gates: margin < gate → plan B (drop lowest science) → plan C (go home)

Terrain is the 30 m HRSC elevation map (ESA Mars Express), checked against Curiosity’s own altitudes in the validation panel; sand is not modelled until the THEMIS layer is connected. The crew visits A, then B, then C; a gate at each stop drops a stop or sends the crew home when the margin ahead falls under the gate. Lowering the safety pad is never offered as a fix.

Where each value comes from

  • Sourced, Apollo [1]: activity rates 700 / 1050 / 1200 BTU/h for riding, station work and walking (p. 1, items 5–7; used here as 0.67 / 1.00 / 1.14 of station work); 20% pad on the emergency walk back (p. 1, item 8); rover 8 km/h nominal and 10 km/h in emergencies (p. 1, item 9); 0.95 h of emergency oxygen for the trip home (p. 1, item 10); 3.1 km walking radius at 3.3 km/h, which the validation panel reproduces (p. 2). [1] applies the 20% pad to the walk back only; LIFELINE also pads the ride back, which is more cautious.
  • Sourced, suit [2]: suit oxygen 8 h: NASA's xEMU lunar suit is specified for EVAs of up to 8 h (6 ± 2 h). It is a Moon suit; no Mars suit figure exists yet.
  • Sourced, radiation [3]: 0.64 ± 0.12 mSv/day dose equivalent measured by Curiosity’s RAD at Gale Crater (shown for context; not used in the margins).
  • Team assumptions, not sourced: walking speed on Mars 2.5 km/h; cooling water 7.5 h (the tightest consumable in the model); the slope slowdown e^(−0.045·slope°); payload effect (−0.5% speed, +1% effort per kg); injured-crew factor (half speed, +30% effort); time in suit 4.25 h; storm warning 30 min. For walking speed, NASA suited tests [4] found a 10 km walk in lunar gravity took 96 min (1.74 m/s), but in Mars gravity subjects neared maximum effort at 1.5 m/s, so a Mars walk must be much slower; 2.5 km/h (0.69 m/s) sits below both, and below Apollo’s 3.3 km/h. The sensitivity chart shows what each assumption costs.
